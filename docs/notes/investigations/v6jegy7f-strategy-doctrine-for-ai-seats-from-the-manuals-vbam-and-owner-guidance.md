# Strategy doctrine for AI seats from the manuals, VBAM and owner guidance

## Question

What should the scripted executor, the teacher and the hands of the learned
seats (t154, t155), know about good play? Which of that does it already do,
and which should it learn next?

## Evidence

### Owner guidance (2026-10-14)

> During the land grab, the AI should build several ETACs and escort ships
> (say one destroyer) before it starts building defensive armies and ground
> batteries. Expansion and exploration (intelligence gathering) is key in
> the early to mid game.

More owner guidance (2026-10-15):

- **Early reconnaissance.** Find the other players' homeworlds and view as
  many worlds as possible early, to learn their colonization status and
  production potential (t176).
- **Pickets.** Station cheap destroyers on Patrol in empty sectors around
  the seat's worlds and on routes between worlds, as an early-warning net
  (t174).
- **Fleets for offence and defence.** Balance them by the tactical
  situation and the seat's goals, weighing risk, reward and return
  (t175).
- **Know when to blitz or invade** (t168).

### The player manual and quick start agree

The [player manual](../../manuals/nc_player_manual.typ)'s Strategy chapter,
"Early Game: The Land Grab", says:

- Build ETACs first, with escorts. An ETAC is reusable and can found a chain
  of colonies.
- Give each ETAC a warship or two and keep the fleet at ROE 0: "its job is
  to arrive, not to fight".
- Choose colonies by Potential and distance.
- Use the speed-6 destroyers as pickets with View a World. A lone scout is
  the hardest fleet to see.
- Then garrison what you take: a handful of armies on each colony, and
  batteries on the worlds you cannot afford to lose.
- Keep taxes at 50% or below while expanding. Above 65% a yearly penalty
  eats into Production.

The [quick start](../../manuals/nc_quick_start.typ) adds:

- Send the two cruiser-and-ETAC fleets to colonize on the first turn, and
  the destroyers to View worlds.
- 50–65% tax is a useful opening range; lower favours growth.
- A starbase at rest in orbit boosts growth below 65% tax, and lets a world
  spend up to five times its production from saved treasury.
- Before assaulting:
  - scout the target and bring escorts;
  - suppress batteries, since surviving batteries block a landing;
  - land with a margin: "Equal numbers are a poor gamble".
  - A captured world earns nothing for two maintenance turns.

### VBAM

*Victory by Any Means*, 3rd edition v1.0.2, is the owner's copy on crunch:
`~/Documents/VBAM-3rd-Edition/`. It is a different game, and a rulebook with
little explicit strategy advice. Its designer's notes are design history.
The points below come from its advisory passages and from what its rules
imply. Quotations are from that edition.

- **Expansion first.** Spend opening resources on reaching and settling new
  systems, not on banked points. Unspent starting points leave you "at a
  military disadvantage compared to your neighbors".
- **Scouts are precious early.** "The loss of one or more Scout fleets ...
  early ... can severely harm a player's ability to expand."
- **Contested colonies.** When two empires colonize one system, both lose
  their colony convoys. NC's reusable ETAC softens this, but racing a rival
  ETAC still risks it.
- **Screening.** "Screening can be an effective way to move civilian units
  such as Convoys out of your task force." NC's warships screen ETACs and
  transports the same way.
- **Fast ships in their own fleets.** "Fleets always move at the speed of
  their slowest ship."
- **Attack with a margin.** In the book's worked example, a 100-point
  attacker loses to 93 points of defence plus a base.
- **Bombard only to suppress.** Bombardment can make worlds "functionally
  worthless", and troops cannot land while fortifications stand.
- **Blockade what you cannot yet invade.** "Blockading a system is an
  effective way to deny resources ... even if you do not have the troop
  strength to try and capture."
- **Garrison everything.** "Place at least some units in every system you
  control", scaled to the world's population.
- **Scout before striking**, to "engage or avoid" enemy fleets.
- **Diplomacy.** A surprise declaration of war angers every other power.
  The book does not cover two-front wars or picking on the weakest
  neighbour.

### Space Empires 4X

*Space Empires: 4X* (GMT Games, designer Jim Krohn) is a board game of
exploration, colony ships and fleets. The advice comes from BoardGameGeek
strategy threads, the Board Game Arena tips page and a review blog, read on
2026-10-14:

- [thread 1555725](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1555725)
- [thread 2828255](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2828255)
- [thread 819523](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/819523)
- [thread 1533801](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1533801)
- [thread 803978](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/803978)
- [BGA tips](https://en.doc.boardgamearena.com/Tips_spaceempires)
- [The Players' Aid](https://theplayersaid.com/2018/04/05/space-empires-4x-from-gmt-games-action-point-1/)

Sources are thin: about 40 strategy threads, mostly about two-player games,
with four-player advice from one thread.

**Widely agreed:**

- **Claim every nearby world before building a military.** "Always colonize
  all non-barren planets in your homesystem. Anything else is suicide."
- **Pre-build colony ships behind the scouts.** "Waiting until you find
  planets to start buying Colony ships is not the best strategy." Every
  turn a colony is delayed costs its growth.
- **Don't risk colony ships in unscouted space.** "Losing a colony ship is
  really painful."
- **Spend; don't hoard.** In SE4X this is a rule: carry-over is capped at
  30 CP.
- **Strike where the enemy is weakest**, deny income, and make him react.
  "Wherever your opponent is weakest, that is where you should strike."
  Count losses inflicted against losses taken, not just ground won.
- **Scout early, toward rivals first**, and accept losses for intel. "Focus
  early exploration on your front row."
- **Answer a rival's buildup at once.** Krohn: "When someone place military
  units in Eco 4, you had better respond."
- **Fortify border chokepoints.** Bases are "most useful on defense,
  especially where enemies must pass through."
- **Don't pure-turtle**; contest the middle ground.

**One player's view, or disputed:**

- The first to take the offensive usually wins. This is disputed: others
  find defence favoured.
- A cheap decoy splits the defence.
- In multiplayer:
  - stay out of the visible lead until you can finish in a turn or two;
  - attacking one neighbour invites the next;
  - strike a rival already engaged with someone else.

**Does not transfer:** the tech race, mines and sweepers, raiders and
cloaking, fighters, and supply pipelines.

### What the executor did (t154, tuned)

- It built armies and batteries first at every world. ETACs and scouts came
  only at home, after the garrison.
- New ETACs flew alone, and colony fleets took the warship ROE (4).
- The starting destroyers gathered at home with the strike force.
- Tax tuned to 64%, and no starbases.
- It bombarded whenever it lacked troops for an invasion, and invaded
  without checking that batteries were down.
- It strikes at 3.4 times a target's estimated defence.

ETAC reuse already works. In 32 executor games, ETAC fleets parked on a
finished Colonize order fell from 73 in year 10 to 1 by year 20.

## Finding and resolution

The land-grab doctrine is now in the executor, on the t155 work tree:

- **Two builds per world per year** (`BUILD_SLOTS`). The second spends what
  the first leaves. Ships finished in one year commission into one fleet,
  so an ETAC and its escort sail together. The action arrays gained a slot
  axis, so learned seats have the same choice.
- **Home build order while worlds remain to colonize:**
  1. ETACs, each with one escort destroyer built the same year;
  2. scouts;
  3. armies and batteries;
  4. starbases and transports;
  5. the preferred hull with whatever budget remains.
- **Colony fleets sail at ROE 0.**
- **Lone destroyers are pickets** during the land grab. One or two
  destroyers alone view the world the seat knows least about.

Tax and starbases stay tuning knobs, and the strategist network sets tax
itself.

## Validation and remaining work

- **The land-grab doctrine against t154's**, in one game through t159's
  switch, on 1,024 maps: one land-grab seat against three classic seats
  wins 0.282, and one classic seat against three land-grab seats wins
  0.250. That is a small but real edge. The doctrine also makes a tougher
  opponent for the learned strategist: the first strategist fell from
  0.568 to 0.489 against it.

- The `nc-ai` tests pass with the new executor.
- **Next:** re-tune the weights by evolution, and check that the land-grab
  executor beats the old tuned one head to head. Then retrain imitation and
  PPO on the new teacher.
- **Candidates, one at a time so each effect is measured.** These are
  t159–t164 in the
  [doctrine follow-up plan](../plans/kvqg7wv5-executor-doctrine-follow-ups-from-strategy-research.md).
  Lessons on tax, war timing and which empire to fight are left to the
  strategist network.
  - bombard until known batteries are gone, then invade;
  - weigh the age of a target's intel in the strike decision, and scout
    alongside the strike rather than before it. Waiting for intel before
    every strike would hold back an aggressive AI (owner, 2026-10-14);
  - blockade a world the strike force can hold but not yet invade;
  - avoid colony targets a rival ETAC is heading for;
  - scale colony garrisons to production;
  - choose strike targets by value over estimated defence, not by value
    and distance alone (SE4X: strike where weakest);
  - scout toward rivals first, and border worlds most;
  - before declaring war, weigh the other neighbours' strength, and prefer
    a rival already at war;
  - a defend posture when scouts see a rival's warships grow.
